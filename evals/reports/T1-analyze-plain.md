# UI/UX audit: Ledgerly static web app (landing, invoices, signup)

**Scope:** `index.html` (marketing landing), `invoices.html` (invoice detail + list, logged-in), `signup.html` (account creation), `styles.css` (shared stylesheet). **Evidence:** code read in full; WCAG contrast ratios computed from the hex values in `styles.css`. **Not rendered:** no browser inspection or screenshots were taken, so spacing, wrapping, and overflow findings are inferred from the CSS. Platform: plain HTML + one CSS file, no framework, no JS beyond inline `onclick`, no existing tokens.

**Assumed top user tasks** (inferred from the landing feature list at `index.html:82-87` and the invoices screen):
1. Understand what Ledgerly is and create an account.
2. Find an invoice and see whether it is paid or overdue.
3. Act on an invoice: send a reminder, mark it paid, download it.

## Verdict

The app fails both lenses at once. A newcomer cannot tell what Ledgerly is: the only description is jargon rendered at 2.3:1 to 2.6:1 contrast, the navigation uses invented names ("The Vault" = invoices), and the real sign-up entry point is a black, unlinked `<span>` at the bottom of the page. Inside the app, every action button shouts in a different color while the one routine action ("Mark as paid") looks disabled, and the signup form asks a "free account" applicant for date of birth, household income, and a card number with no labels and no usable error messages. Underneath is a stylesheet with no systems at all (14 font sizes, 16 spacing values, 10 ad hoc greys, 3 radii, 2 contradictory shadows). The strengths are real but small: a sound system font stack, a viewport meta tag, `lang="en"`, real `<button>` elements in the app, and honest, concrete feature copy hiding at the bottom of the landing page.

## Top fixes (do these first)

1. **Rename the navigation and screen names to plain words, and make them match.** "Launchpad / The Vault / Hive / Pulse / Toolbox" become "Home / Invoices / ..."; the invoices page gets an `<h1>Invoices</h1>` (or "Invoice INV-2041"), and `<title>`, breadcrumb, and panel heading use the same word; signup heading and button become "Create your account" / "Create account". Why: every task starts with finding the right section, and today no label tells an outsider where invoices live (findings 1, 29, 44). Effort: S.
2. **Rebuild the landing hero around one message and one action, and move the feature list up.** Replace "Welcome to Ledgerly!" / "Work. Smarter." / the jargon paragraph with what the features section already says (send invoices, see what is overdue, get paid); one solid primary button "Create your free account" as a real `<a href="signup.html">`, one quiet secondary ("See pricing"); text in white or a light blue tint of the hero hue instead of grey and 45% white. Delete the "About this section" block and cut the promo grid to at most one or two items. Why: answers "what is this / where do I start" and fixes the Critical contrast and dead entry point together, mostly by removing things (findings 7-10, 12-15). Effort: M.
3. **Cut the signup form to name, email, password, with real labels.** Remove phone, address, city, ZIP, date of birth, occupation, household income, and card number (ask later if billing needs them). Give every control a `<label for>`; use `type="email"`, `autocomplete`; remove the "Clear form" button and the "Cancel my subscription" link; replace the static "Error: invalid input." with per-field messages next to the field; delete the instruction paragraph. Constrain the form to about 400 to 480px wide. Why: this form is the single biggest goodwill drain and blocks task 1 (findings 44-57). Effort: M.
4. **Establish a button pyramid and apply it everywhere.** One solid primary per screen (invoice detail: "Send reminder", since the invoice is overdue), outline/muted secondaries ("Mark as paid", "Download PDF", "Duplicate"), link-style tertiary; "Delete" becomes a quiet tertiary and turns bold red only in its confirmation. Drop `.btn-green`, `.btn-orange`, `.btn-grey` as role-less color variants; one radius. Why: fixes the disabled-looking "Mark as paid" (1.68:1), the shouting Delete, the five-way CTA tie on the landing page, and three failing white-on-color contrasts in one change (findings 9, 25, 34, 35). Effort: S to M.
5. **Fix the persistent header.** Brand top-left as a link home; sections next, utilities last and quieter, trimmed to 4 or 5 (move Careers, Press, Partners, Investors, Blog to the footer); an unmistakable current-section style (color + weight, or an accent border) instead of #555 vs #4d4d4d; a conventional search (input with label/placeholder "Search", a real `<button>Search</button>`, no scope dropdown) on the app pages where it is needed; a reduced header on signup. Why: the trunk test currently fails on identity, you-are-here, and search on every page (findings 2-6, 43). Effort: M.
6. **Set the text baseline in `body`.** `font-weight: 400`, `line-height: 1.5`, 16px, very dark grey instead of `#000`; then raise the unreadable greys (`.empty` #aaa, `.footer` #bbb, `.promo b` orange) to a text-safe grey/shade. Why: two lines of CSS improve legibility of every screen (findings 20, 58). Effort: S.
7. **Introduce tokens and snap to them.** CSS custom properties for a spacing scale (4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48), a type scale (12, 14, 16, 18, 20, 24, 30, 36, 48; no em), about 9 greys plus primary/danger/success shades, two shadows from one light direction, one radius; replace most 1px #999 borders with spacing or a background. Add one or two media queries for the header, promo grid, and sidebar layout. Why: removes the root cause of the visible inconsistency (findings 21-24, 26, 27). Effort: M to L.

## System health

Distinct font sizes: **14** (11 px values + 1em, 2.5em, 0.875em, the last nested) · weights: **2** (300, 700; the "normal" one is too light) · colors: **20** (10 of them untinted greys, several near-duplicates; plus 2 rgba shadow colors) · spacing values: **16** px values + em padding · shadows: **2** (contradictory light directions) · radii: **3** (4px, 18px, 0) · line-heights: **1** (1.2 for everything) · border style: **1** (`1px solid #999`, used 14 times) plus one 2px blue.

No systems exist: no CSS custom properties, no scale of any kind. The one accidental consistency is the border, and it is over-used. Consolidate in this order: (1) type scale and weights 400/600-700 with line-heights 1.5 body / 1.25 headings; (2) spacing scale 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48; (3) a grey ramp of 8 to 10 steps with a slight cool tint, one primary blue ramp (pick one of #1a6fe0 / #2456c9 as the 500/600), danger and success ramps with at least 100/500/800; (4) one radius (4px suits the product's plain, businesslike tone), one or two shadows with a vertical-only offset; (5) role-based button classes. Define them as `:root` variables at the top of `styles.css`; there is nothing existing to conflict with.

## What's working

- System font stack (`styles.css:5`): neutral, many weights, no web-font cost. Keep it.
- `lang="en"`, charset, and a correct viewport meta on every page.
- `box-sizing: border-box` globally.
- The app's actions and "Archive" are real `<button>` elements, keyboard operable by default; nothing sets `outline: none`, so default focus rings survive.
- The nav is a real `<ul>` of links with the same five sections in the same order on every page.
- `.quiet-danger` is a genuinely good pattern (low-key tertiary destructive control, 9.28:1); it is just applied to the wrong actions.
- The landing feature bullets (`index.html:84-86`) are concrete and benefit-led; they are the raw material for the new hero.
- The invoice table has the right columns in a sensible order, and the amounts are consistently formatted.
- Status values are words ("Overdue", "Paid"), not color-only dots.
- `.layout` with a sidebar + main split is a conventional app structure users will recognise.
- The fee is at least disclosed; it only needs to move.
